# Demon Killer

**Demon Killer** is an engine-free, top-down Gothic fantasy adventure by **DK Softworks**, built with original pixel art. In the Forsaken Temple opening, the cult sacrifices the bound player, the Devil confronts the emerging ghost, and Lilith leaves a glowing soul crystal after praying at the altar. The cult leader, Devil, and Lilith speak in raised pixel speech bubbles with long, tapered pointers aimed toward the speaking character, keeping the boxes clear of the speaker and the body on the altar. The soul crystal uses original approved pixel artwork at a smaller-than-player scale: its gold bright/dim sprites flicker at irregular intervals while unused, and a successful double-click leaves a single unchanging silver glass crystal. The player can deliberately resurrect or remain a ghost. The sacrifice scene scales the lying body to 40×20 pixels on a centered three-by-two-tile altar (96×64 pixels), matching the smaller surrounding characters. DK Architect uses that same footprint for the translucent preview, rotation, placement, and collision checks.

The game includes character save slots and **DK Architect**, an in-game tool for building and editing the temple.

## Current temple audio and scene

The Forsaken Temple runs the owner-provided Lyon Organ Arrangement MIDI as a browser-synthesized pipe-organ loop when the music preference is enabled. Browsers may require a first click or key press before Web Audio can start. The temple uses a north door that starts closed, opens for the departing cult, closes after the last member, and stays open when Lilith leaves; the Devil vanishes in place. The music is **temporary development/reference material** originating in *Fire Emblem: The Sacred Stones* and must not be included in any commercial release without appropriate music rights or replacement with an original, licensed piece.

The approved NES portrait atlas is stored for later portrait slicing. The approved temple wall atlas is now the source for all 80 directional wall sprites (straight, corner, T-junction, crossing/post and end, each in clean, mossy, cracked and broken variants). DK Architect now uses the owner-approved `assets/art/dkarchitect/ui/Medieval Architect UI Sprite Sheet.png` directly in a compact horizontal, movable Ultima Online-inspired house-building toolbar, replacing the rejected tall tabbed editor. The supplied image provides the DK crest and pixel-art tool buttons; a scrollable horizontal tile strip browses real approved game sprites. Detailed editing controls appear only when explicitly opened. Backup/Restore and Commit/Revert operate on LOCAL browser room drafts; Sync, Clear and multi-level construction are not implemented. It includes a searchable sprite palette, wall-style brush, drag strokes, straight runs, room outlines, selection, object rotation and undo/redo. The approved Sprite Library tab also lets developers position, rotate, move, duplicate and save the available 19 crystal variants and static character art for scene staging. Staged sprites have no actor AI, quest logic or magical effects. The object inspector now supports exact coordinates, cardinal facing, optional non-door collision overrides, copy/paste and draw ordering, all with undo/redo and validated room persistence. The new World Admin and Quest tabs are explicitly marked as future work rather than pretending to have server authority. Existing temple geometry and the resurrection sequence are unchanged. A matching damaged/clean temple floor atlas is planned separately; no new floor artwork has been introduced in this wall build.
